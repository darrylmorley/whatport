WhatPort runs on Intel Macs now.

The app used to refuse to start on anything but Apple Silicon. Intel Macs don't publish the USB-C port-controller data the app was built around, so the gate was there for a reason, but it threw away everything they *do* publish: Thunderbolt ports, the device tree, and power. Those machines now get what they have, with a banner saying what's missing rather than a wall of blanks.

## New

- **Intel support.** WhatPort is now a universal app. On an Intel Mac you get your Thunderbolt ports with link speed and width, the full device tree behind each port, and power. Per-port USB-PD contracts, pin configuration and port health need the port-controller layer Intel Macs don't publish, so those are absent, and the app says so.

- **Energy delivered, per port (Pro).** The port detail now shows how much a port has given the device plugged into it: "1.0 Wh, over 8m at 15.2 W avg". It's per session and resets when you unplug. Flight Recorder recordings carry the figure too, in the session summary and the CSV export.

- **A proper dark-mode app icon.** The icon now has light and dark renditions instead of one plate that looked identical either way.

## Under the hood

- Reduced-detail mode is decided from what the machine actually publishes, one subsystem at a time, and the wording only changes after a reading repeats. A single odd poll can no longer flip the banner on and off, and the data behind it is never held back to make the wording settle.

- The energy figure comes from a firmware counter whose units are undocumented. They were established by measurement (it ticks once a second, and the value is milliwatt-seconds) and the conversion is checked against a known delivery in the test suite. The counter is 16-bit, so past 18 hours the app shows the figure as a floor rather than pretending it's a total.

## Install

Download below, unzip, and drag WhatPort.app to Applications, or:

```
brew upgrade --cask whatport
```

The app is signed and notarised by Apple.
